## Illness and when to stay home {#illness}
<!-- updated: 2026-08-28 by Hannah Lindqvist -->
We follow Washington's child care licensing rule on illness (WAC 110-300-0205). Keep your child home, or expect a call to pick up, if your child has:

- A fever of 101°F or higher (100.4°F for infants under 2 months) together with a change in behavior or other signs of illness, such as sore throat, earache, headache, rash, vomiting, or diarrhea.
- Vomiting two or more times in the past 24 hours.
- Diarrhea with more than two stools above normal in 24 hours, or stool with blood or mucus.
- A rash not caused by heat, diapering, or a known allergy.
- Open sores that can't be covered, or mouth sores with drooling.
- Lice, scabies, or ringworm. Your child may return after the first treatment.
- Signs of serious illness, such as unusual sleepiness, crying that won't stop, or trouble breathing.

A temperature below 101°F without other symptoms does not, by itself, require staying home. Teachers will keep an eye on your child and call you if anything changes.

After a fever, your child may return after 24 hours without a fever and without fever-reducing medicine. After vomiting or diarrhea, your child may return after 24 hours without symptoms. If a doctor diagnoses a contagious disease, we need a note from the doctor saying it is safe to return.

If your child has trouble breathing, a seizure, a severe allergic reaction, or any other emergency, call 911 first.

## Medication {#medication}
<!-- updated: 2026-08-10 by Hannah Lindqvist -->
Teachers give medication only with a signed medication form in the app. Medication must be in its original container, labeled with your child's name, and handed to a teacher. This includes sunscreen, diaper cream, and over-the-counter pain relievers. Children with an epinephrine auto-injector or inhaler need a current care plan signed by their doctor.

## Meals and lunch {#meals}
<!-- updated: 2026-08-10 by Hannah Lindqvist -->
Families pack lunch for children in Chickadees, Ferns, and Orcas. Quail Ridge provides a morning snack at 9:30 am, an afternoon snack at 3:00 pm, and milk with lunch. We offer oat milk for children with a dairy allergy on file.

Packed lunches must be nut-free and labeled with your child's name. We can't heat food, so please send warm food in a thermos. Lunch is at 11:45 am.

**Forgot lunch?** We keep a backup lunch every day for $8, added to your account. Each day there is a main option and an allergy-friendly alternative, listed in the app. Order through the app or tell your child's teacher by 10:30 am.

For Otters infants, families provide formula or labeled breast milk and baby food.

## Food allergies {#allergies}
<!-- updated: 2026-08-10 by Hannah Lindqvist -->
Quail Ridge is a nut-free center. Children with food allergies need a care plan signed by their doctor, which we post in the classroom. Teachers check every snack and backup lunch against each child's allergies before serving.

## Outdoor play, rain and air quality {#weather}
<!-- updated: 2026-09-05 by Hannah Lindqvist -->
We go outside every day, rain or shine, because outdoor play matters for young children. Please keep a labeled rain suit and rain boots at school all year.

We stay inside during lightning, wind advisories, or when ice or snow makes the play yard unsafe.

**Wildfire smoke.** Teachers check the air quality index (AQI) on AirNow before every outdoor time. When the AQI is 101 or higher, outdoor play moves indoors. Families of children with asthma or other breathing conditions can ask us to keep their child inside when the AQI is between 51 and 100. Every classroom has a HEPA air purifier, and we keep windows closed when the AQI is above 100.

**Snow and ice.** We follow Seattle Public Schools (SPS) weather decisions:
- If SPS closes for weather, we close.
- If SPS announces a two-hour late start, we open at 9:30 am.
- If SPS dismisses early because of weather, we close early and call families to pick up.
- If SPS is on break, Hannah decides and posts in the app by 6:00 am.

If a windstorm leaves the building without power or heat, we will call families to pick up.

## Celebrations and culture {#celebrations}
<!-- updated: 2026-08-10 by Hannah Lindqvist -->
Our families come from many cultures, and we love when families share their traditions, languages, and stories with the class. Each year we celebrate the seasons and Lunar New Year, and families are invited to suggest other celebrations.

Because many of our children have food allergies, we celebrate birthdays without food. The birthday child gets a special crown and helper job, and families are welcome to donate a book to the classroom library in their child's name.

## Guidance and behavior {#behavior}
<!-- updated: 2026-08-10 by Hannah Lindqvist -->
Teachers use positive guidance: clear limits, redirection, and helping children name their feelings. We never use physical punishment, shaming, or withholding food, rest, or bathroom use. If a pattern of behavior concerns us, we will meet with you to plan together.

## Communication and partnership {#communication}
<!-- updated: 2026-08-10 by Hannah Lindqvist -->
The brightwheel app is our main way to communicate. Teachers post photos and daily notes and reply to messages during breaks. Conferences are held in November and May. Families are welcome to visit at any time.

## Custody and legal documents {#custody}
<!-- updated: 2026-08-10 by Hannah Lindqvist -->
We follow only the custody and parenting documents on file with us. If a court order limits who may pick up or contact your child, please give Hannah a copy right away. Without an order on file, we cannot keep a legal parent from picking up their child. Staff stay neutral in family disputes.

## Safety, emergencies and reporting {#safety}
<!-- updated: 2026-08-10 by Hannah Lindqvist -->
All staff are trained in first aid and CPR. If a child is hurt, we give first aid, call 911 when needed, and contact you right away. We write an incident report for every injury and share it with you the same day.

We practice fire, earthquake, and lockdown drills every month. Our evacuation site is the nearby community center, and we will call every family if we need to go there.

Washington law requires staff to report any suspected child abuse or neglect to DCYF.

## Concerns and feedback {#concerns}
<!-- updated: 2026-08-10 by Hannah Lindqvist -->
Please start with your child's lead teacher, then contact Hannah if something isn't resolved. You may also contact DCYF's licensing office with any concern.

We keep information about every child and family private. Please don't ask staff about other children or families, and don't share photos of other children taken at the center.
